# Lapis -- Story Bible

> *Ultramarinus*: "beyond the sea".

This is the source of truth for narrative, and it drives world layout. Every landmark, fallen star and constellation placed in the world should trace back to something in this file.

---

## Logline

A silent figure wakes on a moonless beach in a world where the stars are falling into the sea. They believe they are lost and trying to get home. They are actually the memory of a drowned parent, living inside the mind of their daughter as she drifts alone in a lifeboat, and the only way to save her is to guide her home and let her go.

---

## Characters

### The Keeper (player)
- Lighthouse keeper on a remote island. Raised Mara alone.
- Never speaks. Face never shown. Gender unstated.
- Name revealed only in the final shot of the true ending (written in the lighthouse logbook).
- Taught Mara to row, swim and navigate by the stars.
- Invented the bedtime-story archipelago the game takes place in.

### Mara
- The Keeper's daughter, 17 at the time of the storm.
- Clever, restless, desperate to leave the island for the mainland.
- The only voice in the game. All memory lines are hers, at the age of the memory.
- Present in the world as **the distant figure**: seen on far beaches, she vanishes when approached.

### The bright one
- The large four-point star visible from everywhere in the world.
- The Keeper's rule: *"If you're ever lost, find the bright one. Don't lose the bright one, and you'll always get home."*
- In the real world, it is the star Mara is drifting under.

---

## What actually happened

1. The Keeper rows Mara to the mainland: she is finally leaving home.
2. A storm hits mid-crossing. The boat capsizes.
3. The Keeper gets Mara into the boat's tender, pushes it clear, shouts *"Find the bright one!"*, and goes under.
4. Mara drifts alone for three nights. Hypothermic, drifting in and out of consciousness, giving up.
5. **The game world is Mara's mind**: the bedtime-story archipelago, rebuilt from memory.
6. Falling stars are her letting go. When the last one falls, she does not wake.
7. The player is not the castaway. The player is the memory of the Keeper that she is holding on to.

## Rules of the world

| Element | Meaning | Gameplay |
|---|---|---|
| Perpetual night | She is drifting at night | No day cycle |
| Falling stars | Her letting go | Periodic event; world dims |
| Sparkles on water | Fallen stars | Fallen-star collectibles hide among them |
| Glowing palms | Starlight in the stories | Brightness tracks biome restoration |
| Constellations (8) | Her will to live rebuilding | Reveal sky navigation, reignite a biome |
| Lighthouses (1 per biome) | Home; the Keeper's duty | Save points, fast travel |
| Dark Water | Her despair | Spreading starless water; fog; islands sink |
| Waking on the first beach | The Keeper pulling her back again | Failure/respawn state |
| No minimap | Navigation by stars | Players steer by constellations and the bright one |

### Dark Water
- Black, starless, reflectionless water that spreads across the sea over time.
- Sparkles go out, palms grey, fog thickens, low islands submerge.
- Retreats as constellations are restored.
- Linger in it too long: fade out, wake on the first beach. Explains "you've been here before".

## Twist foreshadowing (must be in from day one)

These clues are non-negotiable. The twist only works if a second playthrough reveals them all.

1. **No reflection.** Everything reflects in the wet sand and water except the player. Reflection appears only after the Act 3 reveal, and it is Mara.
2. **Small footprints.** First beach: a line of footprints, smaller than the player's, walking straight into the sea.
3. **The note.** "Don't lose the bright one." In handwriting imitating the Keeper's, the way a child copies a parent.
4. **Memory viewpoint.** Every memory is seen from outside the Keeper, at Mara's eye height, which rises as she ages through the game.
5. **The distant figure.** Seen on far beaches, vanishes when approached.
6. **No cold.** The player walks out of the sea soaked and nothing happens. Late game: frost forms on the inside of the boat, not on the player.
7. **The carved seat.** Initials in the boat seat: Mara's, with the Keeper's beside it, carved by a child.

## Structure

Memory age progression: Coral Shore (age 5-8) -> Sparkling Lake (9-12) -> Karst Peaks (13-17) -> River of Light (the storm night).

### Prologue -- the beach
- Storybook on black, lines accumulating like a page: "Every night, she asked for the same story. / The one about the islands where the stars fall into the sea, / and the palms glow pink from drinking their light. / It always ended the same way. / If you're ever lost, find the bright one."
- First person: on your back in the surf, looking straight up. A star falls and lands on the sand in front of you; its light rises.
- You sit up facing the sea: the fallen star, the footprints, the boat's lantern, The Boat faint overhead.
- Discover: the boat with the carved seat, the note, the small footprints into the sea.
- First falling-star event teaches that the sky is going out.

### Act 1 -- The Coral Shore (refs 2, 3)
- Glowing coral palms, mirror-wet sand, gentle surf.
- Learn walking, rowing, boarding/leaving the boat.
- Constellations: **The Boat**, **The Palm**.
- Biome lighthouse: small harbour light on the headland.

### Act 2 -- The Sparkling Lake (ref 4)
- Vast inland lake ringed by peaks; the densest sparkle field in the game.
- **Home lighthouse** on a central island, visible from everywhere in the lake.
- Constellations: **The Lantern**, **The Oar**.
- **Midpoint set piece**: relight the home lighthouse. The beam sweeps the lake and, for one sweep, lights the distant figure looking straight at the player.

### Act 3 -- The Karst Peaks (ref 1 upper)
- Snow-dusted karst towers; climbing for vantage and sky reading.
- Dark Water accelerates; islands go under.
- Constellations: **The Gull**, **The Wave**.
- The wreck of the real boat, wedged high in the rocks.
- **The reveal**: the capsizing memory, seen through Mara's eyes. The player looks down into the water and has a reflection for the first time. It is Mara.

### Act 4 -- The River of Light (ref 1)
- The Milky Way, as a river of fallen stars flowing between the peaks toward the bright one.
- No longer trying to get home: taking her home.
- Constellations: **The Hand**, **The Bright One**.

### Ending -- the choice, by action
- The river ends at a shore of light. The boat will not move with the Keeper in it.
- **Let go**: step out and push the boat into the light (same motion as the capsizing memory). It carries on alone. Cut to Mara gasping awake in the lifeboat under the real bright star; a ship's searchlight on the horizon.
- **Hold on**: stay in the boat. It drifts back down the river; stars go out one by one; fade to black. Not a game over, an alternative ending.

### Epilogue (true ending)
- The first beach again, same opening shot, but the sky is completely full of stars.
- Years later, real world: an older Mara climbs a lighthouse stair, lights the lamp, and writes the Keeper's name in the logbook. First and only time the name appears.

## Tone rules

- No dialogue trees, no NPCs, no exposition. Story through memory, environment and sound.
- Minimal text: notes and single lines only.
- The world's brightness is the progress bar.
- Quiet, lonely, beautiful. Dread comes from loss, never from threat.
- No combat. No death screen.

---

## Production notes

- **Voice**: one actor (or age-matched actors) for Mara, ~60-80 lines including set pieces.
- **Twist integrity**: never show the Keeper's face, never show the Keeper's reflection before Act 3, never name the Keeper before the epilogue.
- **World layout**: constellation stars cluster by biome; the bright one sits above the far end of the River of Light and is visible from every point in the world.
